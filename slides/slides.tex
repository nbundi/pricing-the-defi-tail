\begin{frame}{DeFi removes many risks --- operational risk is not one of them}
  DeFi's architecture genuinely removes several bank-balance-sheet risks:
  smart-contract custody replaces the custodian, settlement is atomic,
  counterparty credit risk collapses into overcollateralization.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Operational risk remains --- losses from failed processes,
          people, systems and external events --- and in some cases
          concentrates: contract code, admin keys, oracles, economic design.
    \item Unlike a bank, DeFi is infrastructure: no regulatory capital
          requirement, no deposit insurance, no resolution authority. Nothing
          stands between a loss and the depositor's claim unless a protocol
          chooses to put it there.
    \item Yet DeFi markets earn products --- lending supply, yield vaults,
          stablecoin positions, crypto neobanks, etc. --- with minimal risk
          disclosures.
  \end{itemize}
  \begin{takeaway}
    Not whether the risk exists --- it does, and it is measurable --- but
    \emph{who bears it} and \emph{whether anyone priced it}.
  \end{takeaway}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Two responses, neither disciplined by a regulator}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{The protocol can hold a buffer.}\\[2pt]
      Aave's Umbrella
      module, the Sky surplus buffer and Compound's reserve factor are the
      DeFi's analogue to Basel capital reserves --- just voluntary and sized 
      by vote, not by any loss model. \src{BCBS 2006, 2017}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{Or the depositor demands a premium.}\\[2pt]
      A supply yield above the risk-free rate compensates the depositor for
      bearing the tail --- the market-discipline mechanism by which uninsured
      bank creditors price institutional risk.
      \src{Flannery 1998; Egan et al.\ 2017}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vskip 6mm

  \begin{block}{Research question}
    Is either response sized to the operational-risk tail it is meant to
    cover? Answering it needs a common benchmark --- so I first have to
    quantify the tail.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Contributions}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \hl{A consolidated event dataset} --- seven public feeds,
          deduplicated, filtered to depositor-facing DeFi losses, tagged by
          sector and Basel Level-1 event type.
    \item \hl{A per-sector Basel LDA}, fitted as a bank supervisor would, and
          benchmarked against banking operational risk.
    \item \hl{Both margins tested} against that one benchmark: protocol
          capital buffers and depositor risk premia.
    \item \hl{Policy discussion} --- the standardized loss, yield and
          buffer disclosure a depositor needs in order to price the tail
          at all.
  \end{enumerate}
  \begin{takeaway}[Headline]
    Tails comparable to banking operational risk in four sectors and to cyber
    losses in three. Buffered venues cover $\sim 5\%$ of their modeled
    $\VaR_{99.9}$ on average; unbuffered venues pay a higher yield than
    buffered ones, but it covers only $\sim 1\%$ of their $\VaR_{99.9}$.
  \end{takeaway}
\end{frame}

\section{Method}
% =====================================================================

\begin{frame}{The loss-distribution approach, per sector}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{Severity} --- peaks-over-threshold GPD on excesses
      $Y_i = X_i - u \mid X_i > u$:
      \[
        g_{\xi,\beta}(y) = \tfrac{1}{\beta}\bigl(1+\xi\tfrac{y}{\beta}\bigr)^{-(1/\xi+1)}
      \]
      The threshold $u$ sits at the quantile $q^*$ where $\hat\xi$ plateaus,
      subject to $n_u \ge 20$ and $n_u/n \ge 10\%$.
      The severity $F_X$ is then piecewise: the empirical body below $u$, the
      GPD tail above it.
      \vskip 3mm
      \textbf{Frequency} --- monthly negative binomial
      $N_s \sim \NB(\mu_s,\alpha_s)$; annual counts as twelve iid monthly
      draws.
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{Compound aggregation}
      \[
        S_{\text{year}} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} X_i,
        \quad N \sim \NB(\hat\lambda,\hat\alpha),
        \quad X_i \overset{\text{iid}}{\sim} F_X
      \]
      $2\times 10^{5}$ simulated years per sector, each event capped at the
      largest single-protocol exposure.
      \vskip 2mm
      $\E[S]$ is the actuarial pure premium;
      $\VaR_{99.9}$ is the capital buffer the Basel AMA would compute.
      Both expressed as a share of trailing-365d TVL --- the on-chain
      analogue of assets on books.
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{From sector to protocol}
  No single protocol records enough events for its own fit --- the same
  problem Basel solves for data-poor banks with a business-line severity
  allocated by an exposure indicator.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Fit once per sector; treat its protocols as sharing that loss
          process per unit of TVL (credibility-theory shrinkage).
    \item A sector event strikes protocol $p$ with probability
          $\text{TVL}_p/\text{TVL}_s$, so
          $\lambda_p = (\text{TVL}_p/\text{TVL}_s)\,\lambda_s$, same severity,
          capped at $p$'s own exposure.
    \item The tail does \emph{not} thin linearly: with the single-loss
          approximation
          $\VaR_{99.9}(S_p)\approx F_X^{-1}(1-0.001/\lambda_p)$ scaling as
          $\text{TVL}_p^{\hat\xi}$, $\hat\xi<1$, the VaR-to-TVL ratio
          rises as a venue shrinks.
  \end{itemize}
  \begin{takeaway}
    Each venue inherits its sector's loss process in proportion to its TVL,
    which is what makes a per-protocol figure possible without a per-protocol
    history. And because the tail does not thin linearly, that figure has to
    be simulated venue by venue rather than scaled from the sector ratio.
  \end{takeaway}
\end{frame}

        \item Bridge, Derivatives, Other look larger ($23$--$32\%$) but only

      \end{itemize}

  \begin{pitfall}[The familiar pathology]
    The heaviest-tailed, most consequential sectors are exactly the ones the
    model cannot pin down unaided --- the instability that retired Basel's
    internal-model AMA in 2017.
  \end{pitfall}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Sensitivity analysis}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{Sensitivity to the largest events.}
      Dropping the single largest event moves $\hat\xi$ by $0.16$--$0.5$,
      most sharply in Lending ($0.75 \to 0.35$). Under any drop-top rule up to
      $0.5\%$, every sector keeps its below / above infinite-mean
      character.
      \vskip 3mm
      \textbf{Sensitivity to the cap.}
      The cap binds: removing it lifts Lending $18 \to 22\%$, DEX
      $14 \to 46\%$, Yield $15 \to 30\%$. Only Stablecoin is
      cap-insensitive. The $\hat\xi>1$ sectors are stable only under the cap
      --- uncapped, Bridge diverges to $673\times$ TVL and Derivatives to
      $638\%$.
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{Sensitivity to the confidence level.}
      At $99.5\%$ and $99\%$ the Lending requirement falls to $7\%$ and $5\%$
      of TVL, and buffered-venue coverage rises from $5\%$ to $18\%$ and
      $31\%$.
      \vskip 3mm
      \begin{takeaway}[What survives, and what does not]
        The $\VaR_{99.9}$ \emph{level} is genuinely sensitive --- most of all
        to the cap. What does not move is the conclusion: every sector keeps
        its tail character, and removing the cap only raises the requirement,
        so both margins stay an order of magnitude short under all three
        tests.
      \end{takeaway}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

      \midrule

    \end{tabular}

    All four buffers cover the pure premium --- but fall well short of the

    banking regulation asks a bank to hold capital against.
    \src{BCBS 2006, 2017}
  \end{takeaway}
\end{frame}

    \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \small
  Risk premium $=$ trailing-30d TVL-weighted supply APY minus the $3.70\%$
  T-bill. Unbuffered venues average $+54$~bps --- about their $55$-bps pure
  premium, but only $1$--$4\%$ of the tail. Protocol and depositor alike size
  to an average year, not the extreme one.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Both margins together}
  Because buffer and premium substitute, an efficient market would trade one for
  the other --- buffered venues paying less. The data run in that direction.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Median premium: $-86$~bps with a buffer against $+39$~bps without ---
          a $125$-bps gap. One-sided Mann--Whitney $p=0.01$, and
          $p=0.03$ extending to all Lending venues with net TVL
          $\ge$ USD~$100$~m ($n=19$).
    \item Below that size the signal attenuates: posted yields are dominated by
          airdrops and incentives. The long-tail depositor is chasing
          incentives, not pricing risk.
  \end{itemize}
  \begin{pitfall}[Right direction, wrong magnitude]
    The whole premium cross-section spans $418$~bps, while the tail asks for
    about $18\%$ of TVL. The market prices operational risk --- and still leaves
    the tail overwhelmingly unfunded on both margins.
  \end{pitfall}
\end{frame}

\section{What follows}
% =====================================================================

\begin{frame}{Policy}
  \vskip -2mm
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{The shortfall is not evenly borne.}
      \small
      \begin{itemize}
        \item A professional participant can assess the tail, negotiate
              bilateral terms through governance, and size or exit the
              position.
        \item A retail depositor sees only the posted rate and bears the
              unpriced tail disproportionately.
              \src{Cornelli et al.\ 2025}
        \item Market discipline presumes the depositor \emph{can} price the
              risk. For retail that presumption fails twice: the information
              is not disclosed, and pricing a heavy tail takes skills they do
              not have. \src{Flannery 1998}
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{Disclosure levels the playing field.}
      \small
      \begin{itemize}
        \item Capital mandates have no clear path onto autonomous protocols
              --- and supervisors themselves retired the internal-model
              approach. \src{BCBS 2017}
        \item Standardized loss-and-yield disclosure --- a Pillar~3 analogue,
              per sector, alongside the posted yield.
        \item Buffer-and-tail disclosure --- the buffer, the share of
              $\VaR_{99.9}$ it covers, the residual the depositor bears.
        \item The duty attaches to whoever fronts access: front-ends, wallets,
              aggregators, custodial products.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Conclusion}
  \vskip -3mm
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{What I find.}
      \small
      \begin{itemize}
        \item A measurable tail: USD~$9.45$~B over $1{,}075$ events; four core
              sectors no heavier-tailed than banking, three past the
              infinite-mean boundary.
        \item Calibrated as a supervisor would, the Lending tail asks for
              $\sim 18\%$ of TVL.
        \item Against it, buffers cover $5\%$ and unbuffered venues pay
              $+54$~bps --- short by an order of magnitude.
        \item The two margins move together, so the market discriminates; it
              just does not price.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{Limitations.}
      \small
      \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Data.} Publicly reported losses only, and gross of
              recovery --- so the estimates are conservative.
        \item \textbf{Homogeneity.} One severity per sector, allocated by TVL
              share.
        \item \textbf{Identification.} Buffered venues are also the largest
              and oldest, and the yield spread is reduced-form --- an
              association, not an identified effect.
        \item \textbf{Stationarity.} 2020--2026 fitted as one regime.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \begin{takeaway}[The recommendation]
    Not capital mandates, but disclosure: standardized loss, yield and
    buffer-coverage information, owed by whoever fronts access.
  \end{takeaway}
\end{frame}
